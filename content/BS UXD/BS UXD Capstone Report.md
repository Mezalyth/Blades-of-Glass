**Introduction**

This project aims to enhance the PeopleGrove experiential learning platform's usability and accessibility by putting into practice the methods, lessons, and feedback gained over the history of the WGU BS UXD course. After some initial site exploration, a thorough reading of the provided documents, and a brief discussion with the primary contact, Paurav Surenda, I got to work.

The first part of this process would be to fully understand PeopleGrove's use cases, desired functionality, and pain points. Next, create an aggregate persona to group together the demographic for users. Then, define a test a goal, keeping it simple and measurable. After proctoring the user tests and gathering feedback, I created an empathy map designed to organize the data and identify pain points. Lastly, considering the user's experiences, I designed an updated wireframe to streamline the tested process and minimize the identified pain points.

**Testing Notes**

There were a few explicit frustrations that were communicated, and a few assumptions made before organizing the test data into an empathy map. Primarily, there was a lack of visual cues and guidance. Users became lost and were unable to identify where to find projects, and the variety in design patterns were too scattered to create any sense of cohesion or precedence in how elements of the website functioned.

**Design Audit**

In consideration of the data from user testing, here are some things to consider for the design of the website, with the goal of accessibility and usability in mind:

**Homepage - Hero Section**

The hero section is the most crucial part of any website. It's the first impression, and must make an impact. Currently, emphasis is placed on the quantity of projects a user is involved in as well as the completion of their user profile. There's also a large amount of padding that pushes the rest of the website down.

**Homepage - Projects Section**

The projects section fades into the bottom of the screen, just above the fold. A mint background with magenta text is a color combination that fails the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) with a contrast ratio of 2.92:1.

As we scroll, the project card comes into view. The information seems placed, rather than structured with intent. The project dates are top-aligned with the "In Progress" pill, which has more emphasis than the white ghost button that says "View". This particular section gave users considerable hesitation.

**Homepage - How It Works Section**

The value prop at the bottom of the page, where the colors almost "vibrate" to the user's eye. Here is where the process of the experiential learning that PeopleGrove offers is explained. Below that is an image of students with laptops, albeit distorted.

The placement of this section was confusing. At the bottom, it almost seems as an afterthought as opposed to an introduction.

**Project Page - Hero Section/Documents Section**

The main focuses here are Details and Chat. In the top right is a cluster of elements with heavy visual emphasis: the user profile initials in a colored gradient circle, a "Switch to" button, a question mark icon, and an "In Progress" pill. The documents here are listed with ample padding that takes up precious vertical space for a website. The file type icons are a nice touch for quick identifcation.

**Project Page - Deliverables**

Further down is the section for file uploads. The user's name is also displayed here, which seems superfluous. The container for file uploads matches exceptionally closely to a document downloads tile, the only visual cue that this element is different being an inline text styling of magenta. One frustration is that users can only upload one document at a time, rather than a batch of multiple. Another extra step, is that after a file is uplaoded, users must click "Save" to confirm the upload.

**Additional Considerations**

Some considerations that could help the usabilty of PeopleGrove is to give consistent styling to visual elements. Have clear typographical hierarchy where headlines, subheadlines, body copy, and captions are clearly and consistently represented based on typeface weight and size.

In addition, a color palette that's compliant with WCAG, and something more in-brand with the deep blues and greens of PeopleGrove would help break up large sections of the website easily.

Lastly, bringing it all together with structure and alignment would make the elements flow nicely and in expected, conventional ways. Grid layouts and following the fundamental design principles of hierarchy and proximity would group sections of similar information together in a way that's not only pleasing to the eye, but functional and accessible.
